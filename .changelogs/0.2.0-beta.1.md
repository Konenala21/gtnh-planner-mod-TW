We're moving from alpha 5 to beta 1. Most of this release is a rebuilt solver and a lot of speed work. It should hold up for real use now. Back up `gtnhplanner/plans.dat` anyway.

Runs on every GTNH 2.9 build from beta-1 to RC-2. Not 2.8. Stay on 0.2.0-alpha.1 there.

Found a bug? Hit the red bug key on the planner's top bar or post in [our thread](https://discord.com/channels/181078474394566657/1531402304530682036) on the GTNH Discord.

## Better

- The solver buys raw materials and runs your machines instead of buying parts you can make. Spare gets sold as late in the line as it can.
- Machines nothing needs sit at 0 and ask for a count. Old plans may show a few of these.
- Solving is much faster. The same plan gives the same numbers every time.
- No more freeze after every edit. Saving happens in the background.
- Scrolling a count updates the plan as you scroll.
- Thousands of cards pan and zoom smoothly. Wires stop flickering when zoomed out.
- Dragging a wire lights up every card and port that can take it.

- Rates you set on ports show in the overview.

## Technical notes

Engineering detail on what changed between alpha 5 and beta 1. You don't need any of this to use the mod. Timings are from our dev machine.

### Solver

The balancer is a chain of LPs (ojAlgo) over machine rates in crafts per second. Every wired resource network gets a conservation row. Pins become bounds on a machine count or a port rate. Recipe ratios almost never close exactly. An electrolyzer gives H and O at 2:1 and the next machine wants 1:1. So every network also gets an import gate and a surplus gate where the mismatch enters or leaves the plan. Choosing which gates open is the real problem. It is also where most of the bugs were.

#### What was broken in alpha 5

- **Wall-clock budgets changed answers.** Alternatives had 750 ms. The whole solve had 20 s. Each model had its own time limit. The gate MILP's "proven optimal" flag depended on how far branch and bound got before the deadline. A loaded CPU found fewer alternatives and a saved leftover split got spread over whatever was found. Same plan. Different numbers.
- **Alternatives never showed up on real plans.** The enumerator seeded its list with one "current" row per open gate and stopped at 8. With 8 or more open gates it returned only current rows. It still ran up to 1,024 swap LPs first. Every corpus plan past toy size hit this.
- **The whole plan was one model.** One infeasible subsystem blanked every count on the board. The zero threshold for gate support was relative to the largest flow in the plan. A small line next to a big one could lose its gates to that. Machine floors came from the smallest machine anywhere. Pin conflicts were diagnosed plan-wide. Cost grew about quadratically. Ten tiled platlines took 68 times as long as one.
- **ojAlgo's presolve rejected feasible gate sets.** It was also 85% of solve time because it works in exact BigDecimal arithmetic. The big-M gate MILP reported 2 gates optimal where 1 was feasible. On another run it said nothing was feasible.
- **Ties were path dependent.** They went to whatever vertex the simplex landed on. Turning presolve off changed 230_platline's answer.
- **The "every machine runs" floor was 1e-7 crafts/s.** On a plan moving thousands per second that sits under simplex tolerance. The result was noise.
- **Solves were never cancelled.** An edit made during a 30 s solve waited for it to finish and then threw it away.
- **Snapshotting was O(ports × resources)** ore dictionary comparisons on the client thread.

#### What we did, in order

1. **Harness first.** A corpus of real plans exported from the game (`call solvedump`). A golden of each plan's whole answer. A timeless mode so no clock touches a test. Every later step had to reproduce the goldens or justify the diff.
2. **Parallel evaluation.** Independent LPs (alternative swaps and filter trials) run on the solver's own pool in speculative batches. Results are read in submission order so output is identical to a serial run on any thread count. 4–5x on leftover-heavy plans.
3. **Exact gate count.** The big-M MILPs were replaced with `GateSearch`. Needed gates first. Then a reweighted-L1 start and a canonical deletion filter for a minimal set. Then cores and a least hitting set to prove it minimum. Every step is an LP feasibility query. Presolve is off. Every model runs through `Solver.run`. A point must conserve relative to each port's throughput. "Infeasible" is only believed when a Ruiz-scaled revised simplex and ojAlgo's default both say so. Ties break on fixed hashed weights per index. `SolverPathTest` rebuilds every model with its variables reversed and requires identical output.
4. **Per-leftover alternatives.** Each leftover tries every closed gate in its place through the canonical point and keeps its best eight. Needed gates are skipped because they can't move. Swaps are counted instead of timed.
5. **Islands.** Connected components over wires, capped shared machines and drawers. Each island solves alone and in parallel. The results merge into one view whose wiring notes still look across islands. `Balancer.Cache` keys islands by snapshot. An edit re-solves one island and undo is a cache hit. `SolverIslandTest` requires every copy of a tiled plan to match the single plan exactly.
6. **No clocks.** Work is counted. Each model keeps a 30 s safety timeout that no working model reaches. A newer edit stops the running solve between models and keeps finished islands cached. Snapshot matching goes through an index on item id + damage, ore ids and fluid.
7. **ojAlgo 57.3.1 → 57.4.0** for its sparse dual simplex. Identical answers on every golden. A 490-machine connected island went from 13.4 s to 1.4 s. Warm restarts through `IntermediateSolver.update` did not pay off. ojAlgo rebuilds after any infeasible result and most gate queries are infeasible by design.
8. **A new objective.** With the gate count exact we could see that it was the wrong thing to minimize. Fewest gates on palladium_line gave 2,717 machines and 168,000/s traded. The old heuristic gave 421 machines and 12.6/s. Least material and downstream-first rankings turned off whole branches and bought their parts. Forcing every machine on gave 8,305 machines. What works is lexicographic on machine steps:
   - Every port gets a step count. An input counts the fewest machines the plan needs to make that good. An output counts the most machines it could still pass through. Cycles collapse to one step (Tarjan's SCC) so recycle loops of H, Cl or NH3 still trade sensibly.
   - `TradeStage` runs one LP per level. Buys first with the most steps first. Then sells with the most steps ahead first. Each level's optimum is fixed as a bound before the next one.
   - Then least internal flow. Then the canonical tie-break.
   - Anything under 1e-11 of the plan's largest rate is committed as exactly 0. Idle machines read 0 instead of x<0.001.
   - Machines nothing pinned feeds or needs stay at 0 and are reported back so the board can ask for a count.
   - `GateSearch`, the least-quantity stage and the machine floors are deleted. No hardcoded small number decides an answer anymore.

   `TradeRuleTest` pins the rule down with a bread chain (farm → mill → oven). Short on wheat it buys wheat and runs the mill. It never buys flour. Spare wheat is milled and the flour sold at the oven.

At 19,600 cards one more thing showed up. Each choice rebuilt a plan-wide key and that was 87% of a 186 s solve. Choices now keep their island's key. The cache now holds exactly the last two solves' islands instead of a fixed 256 entries that a 400-island plan thrashed.

### Frame time

19,600 cards (Platline tiled 400x) and 9,800 machines placed in the world. Frame cap off.

| | alpha 5 | beta 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board at zoom 1 | 1.1 fps | 135–165 fps |
| Board fully zoomed out (~1,000 cards on screen) | ~3 fps | ~60 fps |
| Overlay in the world | 2.2 fps | 165–185 fps |
| First open in a session | 17 s frozen, 23 s until smooth | 5 s frozen, 11 s until smooth |

- ModularUI walked every child on the board every frame. `BoardCanvas.getChildren` now hands it only the cards and drawers in view from a 512-unit spatial hash plus whatever is held.
- NEI lays out every frame and our layout style asked for a full board relayout each time. It now relayouts only when NEI's panel or the screen changes.
- The overview builds a per-resource index once per plan state and draws only visible rows.
- Wire geometry is rehashed only when something moved. Hover hit tests and wire hops use a segment grid. Far out every wire keeps 1.5 screen px and drops its shadow and arrows. That fixed the flicker.
- Zoomed-out names are placed once against grids and drawn in two batches.
- The world overlay indexes placements in 16-block columns. Multiblock ghosts are captured once per structure into GTNHLib vertex buffers and frustum culled. Display lists are not an option here because block rendering into one comes out invisible under Angelica.
- Loading a save added each wire and drawer by scanning all the others. That was O(n²). It is indexed now.
- The machine maths' name normalizers are regexes. They are memoized per name now.
- Tried and dropped: display lists for zoomed-out card faces (Angelica replays them no faster) and render-to-texture board tiles (175 fps zoomed out but about 500 lines of FBO code for a rare case).

### Edit latency

Every edit serialized every plan on the client thread. In a world with 21 plans that was 0.94 s encoding 5.7 MB plus 0.36 s writing a 49 MB debug copy nothing read. 1.4 s per edit. Now each plan's encoded tree is cached and keyed on version, name and camera. Every recorded edit bumps the graph version so only changed plans re-encode. The file is written on a writer thread and replaced whole. Newest save wins and a shutdown hook flushes one still pending. A save now costs the client thread 0.2 ms. Dropping an input into a source drawer is one 78 ms frame. Deleting a drawer is 25 ms.

The scroll wheel applies each notch at once and the solver runs while you scroll. A spin coalesces into one undo step and one save after 0.5 s of no input, or right away before any other edit.

### Pasted plans

Cards used to take NEI's first catalyst for their recipe. That made Basic Extractors into Steam Extractors and every HV single block into LV. Machines now resolve from the recipe map's handler and the card's tier. Steam machines carry no voltage and bill steam in L/s. Found from a player's Cetane-Boosted Diesel report. 8 of its 28 GT cards were on the wrong machine (one was an HV Steam Compressor running 4x too fast). 3 Industrial Farms were dropped. 10 combustion engines came in sized to 20.8.

### World overlay

- Wire labels try positions along their own wire nearest first. Then off the wire with a leader line. They are scored against machine labels, the card, the corner panels and each other. Machine labels are placed first. A label keeps its spot while clear and glides when it moves.
- The game reports 0 stencil bits so the minimap's circle clip fell back to depth. That leaked whole cards past the edge. It uses ModularUI's stencil now.
- Labels are measured once per card. Wire label icons come from the atlas. No text shadows.

### Tests and compatibility

- 614 headless JUnit tests. The machine maths runs against reference goldens (0 mismatches over 42,250 cases). Solver goldens plus the path, island and trade rule tests.
- `compatCheck` resolves every class, method, field and mixin target we use against each 2.9 build's mod list (beta-1, beta-2, beta-3, RC-1, RC-2). One jar covers them all.
- The update check now orders alpha < beta < rc < release. Alpha 5 jars read beta 1 as newer and will offer it.
