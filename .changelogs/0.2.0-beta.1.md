First beta of GTNH Planner: plan GT New Horizons factories in game, from NEI.

**Out of alpha.** The big pieces are in and the solver has been rebuilt from the ground up, so this is the version to try if you've been waiting. It's still a beta: keep a backup of your world's planner saves (`gtnhplanner/plans.dat`, backed up automatically to `backups/` beside it). Bugs and ideas: the red feedback key on the planner's top bar, or [GTNH Planner's thread](https://discord.com/channels/181078474394566657/1531402304530682036) on the GT New Horizons Discord.

**For every GTNH 2.9 build.** One jar runs on all of them, from 2.9.0-beta-1 to 2.9.0-RC-2 (it is made against RC-2's mods: NEI 2.8.155, ModularUI2 2.3.92, GTNHLib 0.11.52, GT5-Unofficial 5.09.54, CropsNH 2.0.134). It is not for GTNH 2.8; stay on 0.2.0-alpha.1 there.

## Better

### Solving

- The planner buys raw stuff and runs your own machines instead of buying parts they could make, and only sells spare once nothing further down the line can use it. Machines nothing you pinned needs sit at 0 and ask for a count.
- Plans solve a lot faster (big ones go from minutes to about a second), and the same plan always comes out with the same numbers on any PC.
- Scrolling a count or rate updates the plan as you scroll instead of after you stop.
- Rates you set on a card's port show up in the overview, and "~ Set" puts a rate on anything nothing sets yet. "(set count)" only nags on the part of the plan that actually needs a number.

### On the board

- No more little freeze after every change (dropping or deleting a drawer, anything): saving happens in the background now.
- Huge plans stay smooth: thousands of cards pan and zoom without slowing down and open much faster. Wires no longer flicker when zoomed far out.
- Dragging a wire lights up every card and port that can take it. Wire arrows are fewer, bigger and lighter.

> 📷 **IMAGE 7 – Wire in hand.** Mid-drag of a wire: the cards and ports that would take it glowing green.

- Steam machines lose the voltage chip (they don't use EU) and show how much steam they burn instead.
- Zooming out switches cards to the simple view a step later, so the text stays readable longer.
- Custom Rate moved into the new Other column in Non-recipe. The plan button on NEI's recipe pages can be restyled by resource packs, like NEI's own buttons.

### In the world

> 📷 **IMAGE 8 – Plan over the world.** A few placed machines with their labels on (Shift + I), wire labels on the wires, and the card popped up over the one you look at (Shift + U).

- The plan over the world is calmer: a machine's card only pops up when you look at it (Shift + U), and each machine gets a small label with its name, tier, power and count (Shift + I). Wire labels sit on their own wire and stay out of each other's way, shown always or only for the machine you look at.
- Machines can show as see-through, solid or not at all, and the keys list in the corner can be hidden. The settings explain every option, with a live picture of what it does.

> 📷 **IMAGE 9 – Settings.** The gear's In the world section with its live picture beside it.

- Every world key (turn, pick up, remove, Focus, Labels) can be changed in Controls. The minimap can be see-through and shows whole cards one zoom step sooner.
- The plan over the world keeps up with thousands of placed machines.
- The tour doesn't touch your GUI scale anymore.

## Fixed

- Pasted plan links and codes get the right machines: no more Basic Extractors turning into Steam Extractors, or HV machines showing up as LV ones.
- Crop farms in pasted plans come in now, and crop cards share to the library properly.
- Generators in pasted plans keep their count instead of getting resized, and cards no longer land on top of each other.
- Placing machines in the world: you can stack the next one on the one you just placed, and you can see which way R turns it. Each machine of a group says which it is (1/4, 2/4...). A placed generator shows its name, not a code.
- The minimap's round edge no longer lets cards poke out past it.
- The rate unit (/min and friends), EU/t or Amps, and Peak or Avg stick when you close and reopen the planner.

## Upgrading

Your plans may come out with different numbers the first time you open them. The planner now decides where leftovers go by a simple rule (buy as raw as it can, sell as late as it can), and a machine nothing you pinned feeds or needs now sits at 0 and asks for a count instead of running at a guess. Pin a count or a rate there and it's back.

## Under the hood

For the curious: what changed in the engine since alpha.5, and why. Numbers were measured on the dev PC.

### The solver, rebuilt

The solver balances every wired resource with a linear program (ojAlgo). Recipes rarely line up exactly (an electrolyzer makes hydrogen and oxygen 2:1, the next machine wants 1:1), so most plans have leftovers: something leaves the plan as surplus or comes in as an import at some wired port. Each such place is a *gate*. Deciding which gates open is the hard part, and it is where most of the bugs were.

**What was wrong in alpha.5:**

- **The clock decided answers.** Several stages ran on wall-clock budgets (750 ms for alternatives, 20 s per solve, a time limit per model, a MILP's "proven optimal" flag that read the clock). A busy PC found fewer options, and a stored leftover split whose place wasn't found that time got spread over the ones that were, so the same plan could show different numbers on different PCs, or under load.
- **Leftover choices never reached a real plan.** The alternatives list was capped at 8 and pre-filled with one "what it does now" row per open gate before any alternative was evaluated. With 8 or more open gates (every plan in our test set past toy size) no real choice was ever offered, and up to 1,024 swap solves still ran first.
- **Unrelated parts of a plan changed each other.** The whole plan was one model. A part that couldn't balance blanked the counts everywhere; a small part next to a huge one could lose its gates, because "zero" was measured against the largest flow in the plan; the "every machine runs" floors came from the smallest machine anywhere; disagreeing pins were blamed across the whole plan. And the work grew with the square of the plan: ten copies of a platline cost 68 times one.
- **The maths library's presolve was wrong for us.** ojAlgo's presolve declared some sets of gates infeasible that do balance, and was 85% of solve time. The gate MILP claimed 2 gates optimal where 1 balanced, and on another run that nothing balanced at all.
- **Ties went to whichever vertex the simplex happened to land on**, so the answer depended on the solve path, not the plan.
- **"Fewest gates" gives absurd plans.** Once the gate count was exact, it turned out the rule itself was wrong: palladium_line came out at 2,717 machines trading 168,000/s.
- **Small stuff:** idle machines read `x<0.001` (rounding dust) instead of 0; an edit made during a long solve waited for that solve to finish and be thrown away; the "every machine runs" floor was 1e-7 crafts/s, deep in the simplex's rounding on a plan moving thousands a second.

**How it works now:**

- **One plain rule for leftovers** (phase 7): buy as far upstream as possible, sell as far downstream as possible, then the least flow inside the plan, then a fixed tie-break. "How far" is counted in machine steps, and a loop counts as one step (Tarjan's strongly connected components), so a GregTech line tied together by recycled hydrogen or chlorine still trades sensibly. It is settled level by level, one LP per level. The test case is a bread chain (farm → mill → oven): short of wheat, it buys wheat and runs the mill, never buys flour; spare wheat is milled and the flour sold at the oven.
- **Islands** (phase 4): machines joined by a wire, a capped shared machine or a drawer form an island. Each island is solved on its own, side by side on the solver's threads, and cached by its snapshot. An edit re-solves only its island, undo finds the old answer in the cache, and a broken island says why while the rest stays solved.
- **Deterministic** (phases 1, 2 and 5): no clock decides anything. Work is counted, not timed, with only a 30 s safety net per model that no working model reaches. Presolve is off; every answer must conserve before it is shown, and "no solution" is believed only when two different simplex set-ups agree. Ties are broken by a fixed scramble of the index. Parallel work is read back in a fixed order, so the answer is identical on 1 thread or 16.
- **Choices per leftover** (phase 3): every leftover the answer shows lists its own best eight places, and a plan where each leftover has only one possible place says so instead of running 1,024 swaps.
- **A newer edit stops the running solve** before its next model, keeping the islands it finished.
- **ojAlgo 57.4.0** (phase 6) and its sparse dual simplex: one connected island of 490 machines went from 13.4 s to 1.4 s with identical answers.

| Plan | alpha.5 | beta.1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platline, 49 cards | 0.99 s | 21 ms |
| Platline ×10, 490 cards | 67 s | 97 ms |
| In game, 490 cards | 34 s | 1.2 s from cold, 0.14 s an edit |
| In game, 1,960 cards | never finished | 1.8 s |
| In game, 19,600 cards | – | 4.4 s from cold, 0.36 s an edit |

How it's kept honest: a corpus of real plans, each with its whole recorded answer (golden), checked on every change; a path test that solves every plan again with every model built in reverse (a different simplex path) and demands the same answer; an island test where every copy of a tiled plan must answer exactly as the plan alone, and the cache must never answer for an island that changed.

### Frame rate on huge plans

Measured on a 19,600-card plan (a platline tiled 400 times) with 9,800 machines placed in the world, frame cap off:

| | alpha.5 | beta.1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board at normal zoom | 1.1 fps | 135–165 fps |
| Board fully zoomed out (~1,000 cards on screen) | ~3 fps | ~60 fps |
| Plan over the world | 2.2 fps | 165–185 fps |
| First open in a session | 23 s until smooth | 11 s |

- Only the cards and drawers in view are UI widgets now (filed in 512-unit cells); ModularUI used to walk every card on the board every frame.
- NEI lays out every frame, and the board's layout style asked for a whole new layout of the board each time. It now does only when NEI's panel or the screen moves.
- The overview works out what sets each resource once per plan state and draws only the rows in view.
- Wires hash their geometry only when something could have moved, hit-test through a grid, and keep 1.5 screen pixels of width far out (that's the flicker fix).
- Over the world, placed machines are found through a spatial index (16-block columns), and multiblock ghosts are drawn from vertex buffers captured once per structure, only those in view.
- Loading a save adds wires and drawers through indexes instead of checking each against all the others.

### The freeze after every edit

Every edit saved every plan on the game thread: 1.4 s in a world with 21 plans (0.94 s encoding them all, plus 0.36 s writing a 49 MB debug copy that nothing read). Now each plan's encoding is kept and only changed plans are encoded again; the file is written on a background thread (newest save wins, and a shutdown hook flushes one still waiting). A save costs the game thread 0.2 ms. The scroll wheel applies each notch at once and the plan solves as it turns; a spin is still one undo step.

### Pasted plans

A card's machine used to be NEI's first catalyst for the recipe, which made a Basic Extractor a Steam Extractor and every HV single block the LV one. Cards are now matched by their recipe map and tier. Found from a player's report on a Cetane-Boosted Diesel setup: 8 of its 28 GregTech cards came in on the wrong machine (an HV Steam Compressor running 4x too fast), its 3 Industrial Farms were left out, and its 10 combustion engines were sized to 20.8.

### Over the world

- Wire labels try every spot along their own wire, nearest first, before stepping off it with a leader line; they keep clear of machine labels, the card, the corner panels and each other, and glide between spots.
- The minimap's round edge is clipped with ModularUI's stencil. The game's stencil buffer reports 0 bits, and the old depth-buffer fallback let whole cards leak past the circle.
- Labels are measured once per card, wire label icons come from the icon atlas, and labels and tags draw without text shadows, so the overlay is cheaper to draw.

### Testing

614 headless tests (the machine maths and power sources against reference values, the solver's goldens, path and island tests, routing, layout, saves), plus a compatibility check of every class, method, field and mixin target the mod uses against each GTNH 2.9 build's mod list (beta-1, beta-2, beta-3, RC-1, RC-2).

## Install

1. Drop `gtnhplanner-0.2.0-beta.1.jar` into your GTNH 2.9 instance's `mods` folder, replacing any older `gtnhplanner` jar (client only; servers don't need it).
2. Open your inventory: the GTNH Planner button is at the bottom left.
